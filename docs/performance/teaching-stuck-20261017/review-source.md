BookStructure separates candidate preservation from chapter and theme selection. The complete candidate catalog C contains every preserved candidate. Each chapter chooses accepted_stop_refs A from C, and then macro_stop_refs M from that chapter's accepted choices: M is a subset of A, and A is a subset of C. Chapter spine.key_stop_ids uses the macro choices, not all accepted choices. Theme generation searches the complete candidate catalog C; chapter macro choices are only seeds, not its search boundary. Theme stages.member_refs T may include a candidate that no chapter accepted, provided that candidate remains in C and the theme's evidence supports its role. Final BookStructure.key_stops contains the union of chapter accepted choices A and theme members T. Throughlines point to the theme members. A candidate absent from a chapter spine is therefore not necessarily deleted or unavailable to a theme. Example: preserve three candidates: Jia says a cache reuses an earlier result; Yi says a changed input can make that old result inappropriate; Bing says invalidation on update restores consistency. The chapter accepts all three but chooses only Jia and Bing for its macro spine. A correctness theme can choose Yi and Bing. Even if the chapter later does not accept Yi, a theme can still inspect and choose Yi from C. If neither chapter nor theme selects Yi, Yi remains a candidate but is not materialized into final key_stops.
